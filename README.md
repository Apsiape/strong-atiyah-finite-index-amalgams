# The strong Atiyah conjecture and von Neumann ranks for graphs of groups with finite-index edge inclusions

Nidhal Mghirbi and Seth Douglas. Preprint, version 2.0.0, November 2026. [Archive DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24356021) (all versions); version 2.0.0: [10.5281/zenodo.24381994](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24381994); version 1.0.0: [10.5281/zenodo.24356022](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24356022)

## Abstract

Let G be the fundamental group of a graph of groups in which each edge group has finite index in both incident vertex groups. If the von Neumann matrix ranks at each vertex generate a discrete subgroup of the real numbers, we prove that the rank group of G is exactly the sum of the vertex rank groups. This gives permanence for the strong Atiyah conjecture with bounded torsion. The proof expresses a rank as a minimum over edge projections and shows that modules over the edge regular closures attain it simultaneously. In the torsion-free case with integral vertex ranks, the affiliated division ring is the universal field of the graph of vertex division rings. For finite graphs of finitely generated free groups, rational matrix ranks are computable. Applications include finitely presented infinite simple groups acting on products of trees and the exact conjectured denominator for every subgroup of GL₂(ℚ). In SL₂(ℤ[1/2]) an integral group-ring element has kernel dimension 1/12, although every torsion-free subgroup of finite index has index divisible by 24.

## Results

Theorem and corollary numbers refer to `paper/main.pdf`. K is a subfield of ℂ closed under complex conjugation, and Λ_K(H) is the additive group generated by the von Neumann ranks of matrices over K[H].

- **Theorem A.** Let G be the fundamental group of a connected graph of groups in which every edge group has finite index in each incident vertex group. If Λ_K(G_v) is discrete at every vertex, then Λ_K(G) is the sum of the Λ_K(G_v). In particular, if d(G) < ∞ and every vertex group satisfies the strong Atiyah conjecture over K, so does G.
- **Theorem B.** Every subgroup H of GL₂(ℚ) satisfies Λ_ℂ(H) = d(H)⁻¹ℤ. The denominators of GL₂(ℚ) and SL₂(ℚ) are 24 and 12, and that of SL₂(ℤ[1/N]) is 12. One element of ℤ[SL₂(ℤ)] has kernel dimension 1/12 in every SL₂(ℤ[1/N]) and in SL₂(ℚ).
- **Theorem 2.1 (analytic edge formula).** For a product of vertex matrices, the rank is the least sum of the two costs over projections in the edge von Neumann algebra. It follows from the atom theorem of Belinschi, Bercovici and Liu; Appendix A gives a second proof for a single amalgam.
- **Theorem 6.2.** In the torsion-free case with integral vertex ranks, the affiliated division ring of G is the universal field of fractions of the fundamental ring of the graph of vertex division rings, and affiliated rank equals inner rank.
- **Theorem 6.4.** For a finite graph of finitely generated free groups, the von Neumann rank of a matrix over ℚ[G] is computable by a uniform algorithm.
- **Corollary 6.7.** Under the stated coefficient hypothesis, which holds for finite-index graphs of free groups, point eigenvalues of matrices over K[G] in the regular representation are algebraic over K.
- **Corollary 7.1.** Groups acting coordinatewise on a finite product of locally finite trees with finite stabilizers of vertex tuples and d(G) < ∞ satisfy Λ_ℂ(G) = d(G)⁻¹ℤ. This covers the finitely presented simple groups of Burger and Mozes and Rattaggi's torsion-free simple group.
- **Corollary 8.1.** Subgroups H with d(H) < ∞ of the norm-one group of a quaternion algebra over a global field, split at at most one archimedean place, satisfy the strong Atiyah conjecture over ℂ; for instance every subgroup of SL₂(k) with k imaginary quadratic.

Prior work, as the paper credits it: Sánchez-Peralta proved permanence for graphs of groups with finite edge groups, and his class T_VLI covers further graphs when the fundamental group is virtually locally indicable; Fisher and Sánchez-Peralta embedded these group algebras in division rings abstractly for arbitrary edge groups; Theorem 2.1 rests on the operator-valued atom theorem of Belinschi, Bercovici and Liu. The applications also use results of Linnell, Jaikin-Zapirain and Kielak–Linton.

## Changes in 2.0.0

The organizing result is now Theorem A, for graphs of groups with finite-index edge inclusions and bounded torsion. It subsumes the amalgam theorem of version 1.0.0, hence the new title. New in this version: Theorem B on GL₂(ℚ), the universal-field theorem for graphs, the exact-rank algorithm, and Corollaries 7.1 and 8.1 in their present generality. The finite verification program of version 1.0.0 checked examples specific to that version; it is superseded here and remains available in the 1.0.0 release.
